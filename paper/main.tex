\begin{abstract}
Natural-language protocol specifications can admit multiple plausible implementations despite using the normative keywords of BCP~14. Detecting ambiguity before an Internet-Draft becomes a Request for Comments (RFC) is methodologically difficult: clauses repeat across revisions and related protocols, positives are rare, and later RFC or erratum evidence can leak into draft-stage prediction. We present \system, a reproducible corpus and modeling pipeline with explicit revision, family, and information-boundary controls. The source corpus covers 253 draft revisions, 12 RFCs, and 31 verified technical errata. In the latest hash-pinned transformer experiment, five-fold family-isolated out-of-fold (OOF) evaluation covers 2,125 development clauses from nine protocol families, including 136 operational-reference positives (6.4\%). A target-only DeBERTa-v3-base ensemble, averaging fitting-side empirical-percentile ranks from focal-loss and partially fine-tuned models, obtains average precision (AP) 0.3711 (95\% family-cluster bootstrap interval 0.3151--0.5084), compared with a 0.064 prevalence baseline. In a pooled OOF review-budget analysis, it retrieves 26 reference positives among the top 50 clauses and 49 among the top 100. A separate three-fold classical/heuristic cohort of 2,406 clauses (158 positives) yields AP 0.522 for logistic regression with engineered and TF--IDF features and 0.105 for the fixed heuristic. A Qwen/hybrid study on a different label artifact reports AP 0.196 for eight-shot prompting and 0.325 for its V4 lexical/semantic hybrid; these values are not directly comparable across cohorts. All metrics are development estimates, not independent accuracy or final generalization results: labels are human/AI-assisted operational references rather than adjudicated expert gold, transformer training uses one seed, and reserved TCP and sealed DNS data remain unopened. We therefore position \system{} as a research-stage review-prioritization aid, not an autonomous ambiguity judge.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) develops protocols through sequences of mutable Internet-Drafts before stable RFC publication. Although capitalized terms such as \emph{MUST}, \emph{SHOULD}, and \emph{MAY} have defined meanings under BCP~14~\cite{rfc2119,rfc8174}, their surrounding prose can still leave actors, conditions, references, failure behavior, or interactions under-specified. Multiple standards-conforming interpretations can then produce interoperability defects or security-relevant implementation differences.

Prior systems show that protocol prose can be analyzed automatically. SAGE combines semantic parsing and author clarification to disambiguate RFC text and generate code~\cite{yen2021sage}. NRFCKG represents protocol requirements as a knowledge graph and searches for contradictions~\cite{feng2023nrfckg}. Most directly, RFCScope derives seven logical-ambiguity subtypes from verified errata and uses an LLM pipeline to find ambiguities in published RFCs~\cite{pawagi2025rfcscope}. These systems motivate automated support, but they do not answer a different operational question: \emph{could a risky clause have been prioritized while it was still an Internet-Draft, using only information available at that time?}

That question introduces three hazards. First, a final RFC, later revision, or later erratum is an informative label source but an invalid prediction-time feature. Second, clause-level random splits place nearly identical text from the same revision lineage on both sides of an evaluation. Third, the positive class is rare, so accuracy and even ROC area can obscure poor retrieval of ambiguous clauses; precision--recall analysis is more informative under skew~\cite{davis2006pr,saito2015pr}.

This paper reports a complete development-stage study of \system. Its central design principle is separation: source collection, structural parsing, revision alignment, candidate construction, annotation, model input, model selection, reserved validation, and sealed testing are distinct, hash-recorded stages. The intended output is not an automatic declaration that a standard is defective. It is a ranking that helps an author or reviewer allocate attention.

We investigate four questions: (RQ1) how do transparent heuristic and classical ML baselines perform on the matched annotation cohort? (RQ2) does target-only clause text rank operational-reference positives above negatives under family-isolated transformer evaluation? (RQ3) how do prompt-only and hybrid language-model configurations behave on their separate label artifact? (RQ4) how many reference positives would a reviewer encounter at fixed review budgets, and how much does performance vary by family?

The contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A revision-aware corpus and clause-lineage pipeline covering 253 draft revisions, 12 RFCs, and 31 verified technical errata, with future-information guards.
  \item Matched-cohort evaluation of classical ML models and a deterministic, interpretable heuristic, alongside a distinct nine-family LOFO baseline study.
  \item A hash-pinned, five-fold family-isolated DeBERTa-v3-base study on 2,125 eligible clauses across nine families, with 136 operational-reference positives.
  \item An OOF comparison of focal-loss fine-tuning, partial fine-tuning, and a fitting-side ECDF rank ensemble, including family-clustered uncertainty and review-budget analyses; and separately reported Qwen-prompt/hybrid results.
  \item An explicit claim boundary: the labels are not independently adjudicated expert gold, the study uses one seed, and reserved TCP/DNS evaluations remain unopened.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{Ambiguity in requirements and protocol text}

Ambiguity detection has a long history in requirements engineering. Rule-based tools provide interpretable indicators but struggle with context; supervised and contextual models can learn broader patterns but inherit dataset definitions and annotation noise. TAPHSIR, for example, combines machine learning for anaphoric-ambiguity detection with a BERT-based resolution component~\cite{ezzini2022taphsir}. A recent hybrid requirements study combines ambiguous-phrase rules with conventional classifiers~\cite{izhar2025ambiguity}. Metrics from such corpora are not directly transferable to Internet-Drafts because the unit of analysis, prevalence, and meaning of ambiguity differ.

Protocol specifications add domain structure: packet fields, state transitions, normative strength, cross-references, and compatibility requirements. SAGE converts selected RFC prose to logical forms, asks humans to resolve remaining alternatives, and generates protocol code~\cite{yen2021sage}. PROSPER uses LLMs to extract protocol state machines from RFC text and diagrams~\cite{sharma2024prosper}. NRFCKG targets direct and conditional contradictions using extracted entities, rules, and constraint solving~\cite{feng2023nrfckg}. These systems address important neighboring problems, but they either require specialized formalization or target a narrower error class.

RFCScope is the closest conceptual predecessor. It analyzes 273 verified technical errata, defines seven subtypes under inconsistency and under-specification, and uses context construction, semantic partitioning, subtype-aware LLM analysis, and validation. Its evaluation reports 31 new ambiguities in 14 of 20 RFCs, including author-confirmed cases~\cite{pawagi2025rfcscope}. We reuse its subtype vocabulary as conceptual guidance, not as an automatic label oracle. Our temporal unit is a historical draft revision, and our present binary task asks whether an ordinary engineer could reasonably interpret a clause in materially different implementation-relevant ways.

\subsection{Revision alignment}

Document evolution has been studied outside networking. arXivEdits provides multi-version scientific papers with sentence alignments and edit intentions~\cite{jiang2022arxivedits}; CASIMIR extends revision corpora at larger scale~\cite{tinnes2024casimir}. Those datasets establish that insertion, deletion, movement, split, and merge must be represented explicitly. In a protocol specification, however, a textual change is not itself evidence of ambiguity: it may introduce functionality, update terminology, or merely edit style. We therefore separate \emph{correspondence} from \emph{ambiguity}, and never use the final RFC or future revision as a detector input.

\subsection{Models for imbalanced text classification}

The present experiment compares two target-only \texttt{microsoft/deberta-v3-base} classifiers and an equal-weight rank ensemble. DeBERTa-v3 uses replaced-token detection and gradient-disentangled embedding sharing~\cite{he2023debertav3}. Focal loss emphasizes hard examples under class imbalance~\cite{lin2017focal}. The partial model freezes embeddings, the shared backbone, and layers 0--7, and trains layers 8--11, the pooler, and classifier. A separate local-Qwen/hybrid line of work is reported below; its different effective cohort and evaluation artifact preclude direct comparison with this transformer study.

\subsection{Collection and structural parsing}

The curated corpus covers application, DNS, routing, security, and transport standards. Twelve RFCs and all available histories for ten modern draft families are stored; OSPFv2 and BGP-4 are RFC-only because their historical drafts are not available through the same archive. The audit reports 253 expected and present draft revisions, 12 expected and present RFCs, and 31 verified technical errata.

The parser accepts both Datatracker plaintext and XML2RFC. It emits a common document--section--block tree while retaining original text, section paths, source locations, reference targets, tables, artwork, and formal syntax. A deterministic clause layer conservatively separates prose and list items. It recognizes uppercase BCP~14 keywords while guarding quoted text, references, abbreviations, version numbers, URLs, and condition--requirement pairs. Each clause receives a stable identifier and one of \textsc{Normative}, \textsc{Contextual}, \textsc{Excluded}, or \textsc{Manual Review}. This stage identifies likely requirements; it does not decide ambiguity.

\subsection{Consecutive-revision alignment}

For revisions $t-1$ and $t$, bidirectional retrieval first searches corresponding and nearby sections, followed by a bounded global fallback. Candidate score $s(i,j)$ combines semantic, lexical, structural, positional, block-type, and protected-token evidence:
\begin{equation}
s(i,j)=\sum_{k} w_k s_k(i,j),
\end{equation}
where semantic and lexical similarity each receive weight 0.38 in the semantic pilot, structure 0.12, and position, block compatibility, and protected-token evidence 0.04 each. Distinctive exact anchors are locked only when supported by section structure. One-to-one components use bipartite assignment with unmatched choices. Competing split and merge candidates of at most three adjacent clauses use a mixed-integer program with non-overlap constraints. Moves require section correspondence or reliable anchor inversions; close alternatives remain \textsc{Uncertain}.

MiniLM embeddings are computed from direct tokenizer-ID windows, avoiding decode/re-tokenize drift. The TLS coverage pilot aligns 30 documents across 29 consecutive pairs: 52,137 clause-version occurrences produce 52,953 relationships, including 4,452 modified, 2,356 inserted, 2,025 deleted, 261 split, 260 merged, 1,808 moved, and 5,199 uncertain relationships. Fig.~\ref{fig:alignment-counts} visualizes these counts. They establish pipeline coverage, not alignment accuracy: the stratified 150-link review sample has not been adjudicated, so link precision and recall are intentionally unreported. Revision links are not model features in the transformer experiment reported below.

\subsection{Temporal and leakage controls}

For a clause in revision $t$, revision features may depend only on revisions $\leq t$. Later drafts, RFC text, later errata, undated evidence, annotation metadata, and model votes are rejected by a feature guard. RFC endpoints are available for offline correspondence research but not for draft-stage prediction. Family grouping blocks related clauses from crossing train and evaluation roles. Exact normalized cross-partition target overlap is zero in the current artifact. Fitting-side exclusions additionally cover near duplicates, shared lineages, BCP~14 skeleton conflicts, and target-as-context reproduction.

\section{Reference Labels and Partitions}

\subsection{Operational annotation reference}

The project annotation workflow was supported by two AI models. The checked-in \texttt{final\_annotation.csv} contains 2,406 labeled clauses (158 positive and 2,248 negative) across 11 draft families; these are the rows used by the matched classical/heuristic comparison. The latest transformer study evaluates its separately frozen eligible subset of 2,125 clauses after family and label-blind fitting exclusions. Labels are an \emph{operational reference}, not independently double-annotated expert gold; results quantify agreement with this workflow rather than universal ambiguity-detection accuracy.

\section{Models and Experimental Protocol}

\subsection{Evaluation measures}

For scores $p_i$ and labels $y_i$, the primary ranking metric is average precision (AP), computed as the stepwise precision-weighted increase in recall. Repository reports historically call this ``PR-AUC''; here we use the more precise name AP. Its no-skill reference is the positive prevalence, 0.064 in the evaluated development cohort. We also report ROC-AUC, precision, recall, $F_1$, $F_2$, balanced accuracy, confusion counts, and retrieval at fixed review budgets. Threshold-free rankings are primary because the appropriate operating point depends on reviewer capacity and the relative cost of missed ambiguity.

Uncertainty for the latest transformer study is estimated with 2,000 bootstrap resamples of the nine RFC families. These intervals represent sampling variation across the observed families; they do not include model-training variability, since the newest study has one seed.

\subsection{DeBERTa variants and rank ensemble}

Inputs contain only the target clause, with a maximum length of 256 tokens and deterministic head--tail truncation; the experiment reports no target truncation. Both models use five epochs, AdamW, learning rate $10^{-5}$, weight decay 0.01, a linear schedule with 10\% warmup, batch size 8, FP32, deterministic execution, and seed 2718. The focal model uses $\gamma=2$ and fitting-side class weights ($\alpha_0=1$, $\alpha_1=N_{-}/N_{+}$):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{focal}}=-\alpha_y(1-p_y)^\gamma\log p_y.
\end{equation}
The partial model uses weighted cross-entropy and trains only layers 8--11, the pooler, and classifier.

For the ensemble, each method's empirical cumulative distribution function is fit on the corresponding fitting-side inner OOF scores. The two percentile ranks are averaged with equal weight and applied to the untouched outer-fold predictions. The resulting value is a ranking score, not a calibrated probability.

\subsection{Classical machine learning and heuristic}

The classical/heuristic comparison uses the separate 2,406-row annotation cohort, with 158 positives (6.57\%) from 11 draft families. Models are evaluated using three-fold stratified group cross-validation, keeping draft-family groups intact; feature scaling, TF--IDF vocabulary, and feature selection are fitted within each training fold. The main logistic-regression model uses 25 engineered clause features concatenated with word unigram/bigram TF--IDF, class-balanced L1 regularization, and $C=0.5$. We compare it with logistic regression on TF--IDF only and on frozen 384-dimensional MiniLM embeddings, and with Linear SVM and random forest using the engineered-plus-TF--IDF representation. This is an exploratory experiment on an annotation artifact distinct from both the newer DeBERTa and the Qwen/hybrid evaluation cohorts.

The fixed heuristic is not trained on labels. It assigns hand-set points to conditional clauses lacking exception markers, deictic expressions, mixed BCP~14 modal levels, subjective terms, and exceptions; numeric bounds and explicit target references subtract points. The score is clipped to $[0,9]$, normalized by nine, and thresholded at 0.4. Its rules and threshold are fixed rather than fit per fold. We report average precision from OOF scores and threshold metrics at the recorded operating threshold; the heuristic is a transparent low-cost baseline, not a learned classifier.

\subsection{Earlier family-held-out classical study}

The repository also contains a distinct earlier study with a nine-family leave-one-family-out protocol, 2,540 evaluated clauses, and 17.8\% positive prevalence. Its combined target TF--IDF/engineered baseline (E0) has pooled OOF AP 0.291 (95\% bootstrap interval 0.260--0.335). Frozen MiniLM and BGE-small representations with engineered features reach AP 0.246 and 0.245, respectively. Simple one-revision predecessor signals yield AP 0.168 and 0.165. These results provide historical methodology context only; their cohort and labels differ from the later 2,406-row ML/heuristic comparison and from the current transformer/LM cohorts.

\subsection{Language-model and hybrid study}

We also report the repository's separate Qwen3-32B development study~\cite{yang2025qwen3}, which uses the frozen \texttt{final\_annotation\_ready.csv} label artifact: 2,142 clauses from nine families, including 126 positives (5.88\%). It uses the same five family-grouped outer folds as defined for that label artifact, but it is not the 2,125-row transformer cohort described above. The model is a local quantized Qwen3 32B inference model; V1 and V2 update no model parameters. V1 compares zero-, four-, and eight-shot prompts, selecting demonstrations from fitting families and scoring the \texttt{yes}/\texttt{no} alternatives from native token log-probabilities. V2 tests stricter prompts with 8, 16, or 24 fold-safe demonstrations, including prevalence-aware class proportions, and estimates threshold operating points by cross-fitting. V3 combines frozen Qwen zero-shot scores with a nested, target-only word/character TF--IDF logistic classifier; its regularization and blend weight are chosen using inner-fold predictions. V4 combines the V3 score with frozen MiniLM semantic representations of the target and local context using the Sentence-BERT bi-encoder paradigm~\cite{reimers2019sbert}. The semantic classifiers use class-balanced logistic regression ($C=0.03$); the 0.80/0.10/0.10 V3/target/context combination was selected on development data. V3 and V4 are supervised hybrid models, not parameter-fine-tuned LLMs. Results for this distinct cohort are reported in Section~\ref{sec:llm-results}.

\subsection{Nested operating points}

Threshold selection is performed only on the fitting side. For each outer fold, three family-disjoint inner folds estimate method-specific thresholds. The policy maximizes recall subject to minimum fitting precision in $\{0.20,0.30,0.50\}$, breaking ties by higher precision and then higher threshold. Infeasible targets remain missing; they are not replaced by an always-positive rule. Precision targets are objectives on inner data, not guarantees on a held-out family.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation of the development results}

The family-isolated OOF scores indicate that clause text contains a useful ranking signal relative to the operational labels. They do not establish that the model identifies ambiguity in an independently verified sense. The focal-loss and partial-fine-tuning models have overlapping family-cluster intervals, and the ensemble's nominal advantage is not a significance result. A second run across multiple seeds and independent expert annotations is needed before choosing a winner.

The separate language-model study shows a different pattern: direct Qwen prompting gives high recall at low precision in V1, while the stricter V2 prompt substantially reduces recall and AP. Adding a nested lexical classifier in V3 and frozen semantic features in V4 improves AP within that study's development cohort. These are adaptive comparisons with an operational label reference; they neither establish that LLMs outperform DeBERTa nor support direct numeric comparison across the two cohorts.

Ranking is more defensible than treating the model as a binary gate. The selected inner-fold thresholds optimize a precision constraint on fitting data, but pooled OOF behavior and family-specific outcomes show that the constraint does not transfer uniformly. A user-facing system should present clauses for expert review, expose source revision and surrounding context, and avoid claims that a low score certifies correctness.

On the separate classical/heuristic cohort, engineered features combined with TF--IDF produced stronger AP than the fixed heuristic and the tested embeddings-only baselines. This supports the value of lexical and explicit clause cues for ranking within that cohort, but the high-recall logistic operating point has low precision and does not remove the need for human review. The earlier LOFO results offer additional historical evidence for sparse lexical features, but differ in annotation, cohort, and validation setup. Likewise, the Qwen/hybrid results cannot be ranked against the DeBERTa or classical models because their label artifacts and evaluated populations differ.

Revision alignment provides useful lineage and leakage-control infrastructure, but its pilot has not been evaluated against adjudicated gold links and its outputs are not used as predictive features in the transformer study.

\subsection{Deployment interpretation}

The recommended future interface is a ranked review queue with the target clause, local context, source revision, score, and an explicit uncertain region. Automatic rejection or publication certification is inappropriate. The global pooled OOF top-50 precision is 0.52 against the operational reference; this is a retrospective development diagnostic, not an estimate of production precision or guaranteed reviewer yield.

\subsection{Reproducibility and governance}

The experiment records hashes for the authoritative label source, effective dataset, model snapshot, folds, configuration, and derived predictions. The source-label SHA-256 is \texttt{8e395e83087c28cdc8e5b514f6fb4eca96e54a31fb525be770810db6f9e22e1d}; the effective dataset SHA-256 is \texttt{19364a405048c279c2959ed6110a2e6868f2b4759ea21c659399038e6fbcec23}. The model snapshot is used offline, and the experiment manifest pins seed, tokenizer, and training configuration. Code and documentation are available in the project repository~\cite{artifact2026}. The latest run artifacts are not yet part of a reviewed, immutable public release.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\subsection{Construct validity}

The binary reference operationalizes ambiguity through a particular rubric and annotation workflow. It does not exhaust RFCScope's seven subtypes, and AI-supported labels can reproduce shared model biases. Qwen3 contributed to annotation support, making the Qwen agreement analysis non-independent of label construction. The manual-conflict process is not equivalent to blinded, independent, double expert annotation. These issues limit claims about the real-world construct of ambiguity.

Candidate selection is also enriched rather than population-random. Metrics estimate performance on the reviewed candidate cohort, not on every sentence or clause in all Internet-Drafts. AP and prevalence therefore must not be transported to the full IETF corpus.

\subsection{Internal validity}

Family splits, fitting-only preprocessing, inner-only thresholds, and exact/near/lineage exclusion reduce leakage. They cannot eliminate pretraining contamination: modern encoders may have seen historical RFCs or drafts. The experiment uses a single seed, and family bootstrapping does not account for training variance. Architecture and analysis choices were made within the wider development process; the reported OOF study should therefore be interpreted as development evidence, not a pre-registered confirmatory benchmark.

\subsection{External validity}

Only nine families contribute scored development rows, and QUIC/HTTP dominate row counts. The smallest families cannot independently support meaningful AP estimates. TCP and DNS are reserved and unopened; the reported study therefore provides no evidence about performance on those families. Routing protocols with historical drafts outside the current cohort, standards from other organizations, and newly authored drafts after the models' pretraining period are also untested.

\subsection{Alignment validity}

The TLS alignment run has deterministic audits but no reviewed gold links. Alignment confidence is rule-based and uncalibrated. Split/merge grouping, move heuristics, and long-clause pooling are approximations. Accordingly, correspondence statistics are engineering coverage, not evidence of alignment accuracy, and automatic revision relationships are not used as ground-truth ambiguity labels.

\section{Ethical and Practical Considerations}

All source standards and errata are public technical documents. The principal risk is epistemic rather than personal: an incorrect ``ambiguous'' flag could waste reviewer time or unfairly characterize an author's work, while a false negative could create unwarranted confidence. The system must expose uncertainty, provenance, and its non-certification role. Generated explanations should be treated as hypotheses and checked against the exact contemporaneous text. Final evaluation should be reported once, including negative outcomes, and the sealed set should not be recycled for iterative tuning.

\section{Conclusion}

\system{} provides a reproducible pipeline and development evidence for prioritizing potentially ambiguous clauses in historical Internet-Drafts. On the latest nine-family transformer cohort, the equal-weight ECDF ensemble reaches AP 0.3711 at 6.4\% prevalence, with a 95\% family-cluster bootstrap interval of 0.3151--0.5084; it retrieves 26 operational-reference positives in the top 50 pooled OOF clauses. On a separate 2,406-clause cohort, logistic regression with engineered and TF--IDF features reaches AP 0.5225, while the fixed heuristic reaches 0.1049. The separate Qwen/hybrid study reports AP 0.1960 for eight-shot prompting and 0.3252 for its V4 hybrid. These results come from distinct label artifacts and evaluation cohorts and are not a head-to-head comparison. Across the studies, performance estimates remain development evidence: outcomes vary by family, the transformer evaluation uses one seed, and the operational labels are not independently adjudicated expert gold.

Before making a deployment or final-generalization claim, the project should: (1) independently double-annotate and adjudicate a stratified expert sample; (2) repeat the selected transformer configurations across multiple seeds; (3) freeze one method, threshold/routing policy, code snapshot, and analysis plan before opening TCP; and (4) after the TCP decision rule is fixed, execute exactly one DNS evaluation. Until those steps are complete, the appropriate claim is a reproducible, promising development-stage review aid---not a published-standard oracle or autonomous quality gate.
